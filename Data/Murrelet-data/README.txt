BACKGROUND

The marbled murrelet is a small seabird found in the North Pacific. It spends most of its 
life at sea and visits old growth forests along the coast during its breeding season.
It is listed as threatened under the Canadian Species at Risk Act primarily due to loss 
of old growth nesting habitat.


This dataset contains information on marbled murrelet counts made using marine radar 
during the presunrise period as they entered and exited the forest. This project began in 
1996 and is ongoing (with a lower level of effort) as of February 2024. Surveys generally 
conducted bi- or tri-annually at each radar station. Long-term radar stations are defined 
as a specific geographic coordinate that are returned to consistently within and across 
years to execute a monitoring survey. Radar stations vary with respect to location to the 
shoreline, but locations are chosen that allow clear, unobstructed detection of the sky; 
any obstructions to the radar are recorded. Radar stations are grouped into six Marbled 
Murrelet Conservation Regions across British Columbia, as defined by the Marbled Murrelet 
Recovery Team (2014). The survey data were used to develop a timeseries of counts used to 
estimate population trends for marbled murrelets in each of British Columbia's six Marbled 
Murrelet Conservation Regions. To estimate population trends in marbled murrelets, see 
methods used in Drever et al. 2021 "Trends in radar counts of Marbled Murrelets 
Brachyramphus marmoratus in British Columbia (1996-2018): Effects of 'The Blob' marine 
heatwave and prey fish abundance" 
(http://www.marineornithology.org/PDF/49_1/49_1_37-49.pdf) and Bertram et al. 2015 
"Estimation of Coast-Wide Population Trends of Marbled Murrelets in Canada Using a 
Bayesian Hierarchical Model" (https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0134891).

Supplementary information regarding this project and data can be accessed on the 
marbled murrelet OSF page: https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/ZSG63. This includes resources 
such as academic publications, published government reports and protocols, and R scripts 
associated with data tidying.

##########

mamu-count-data.csv

This document contains all variables surveyed and their recorded values. Missing values 
are indicated by NA for all variables. 

Key variables for analysis include radar station ID, radar model, tilt, observers, data 
and time, and predawn incoming counts of birds. Importantly, radar stations were modified 
by tilting the radar upward to better detect birds; however, the tilt angle varies between 
years and locations and affects detection capability. Thus, statistically controlling for 
this variable is important to accurate interpreting the data. Observer and Principle 
Investigator (PI) are anonymized to protect privacy, but may also be statistically 
controlled for.

##########

mamu-count-metadata.csv

This document contains descriptions of all variables in the count dataset and describes
characteristics such as acronyms and variable units.
